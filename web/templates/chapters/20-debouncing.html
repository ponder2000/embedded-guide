SECTION 01

One press. Many edges.

A mechanical switch is not an ideal digital source. Its contacts can make and break several times before settling. A GPIO interrupt faithfully reports those edges—even when a person intended only one press.

Start with the GPIO lesson and the first hardware project. Verify your switch's wiring, active level, and pull resistor. Normalize its electrical level to pressed = true before applying a debounce algorithm; pressed does not universally mean low.

These are illustrative waveforms, not timing specifications. Bounce duration varies with the switch, age, wiring, and noise. Debouncing does not protect a pin from overvoltage, ESD, or unsafe wiring.

SECTION 02

Accept a state, not every sample

One simple sampled filter remembers a candidate state and counts consecutive observations of it. Accept that state only when the count reaches a threshold. Generate a press event only on the accepted transition from released to pressed; remaining held must not repeatedly fire the event.

The initial accepted state here is released. In production, choose an explicit boot policy: read and qualify the initial input, or deliberately require a release before accepting a first press. A button held during reset must not surprise the application.

SECTION 03

Keep the loop available

With nominal 5 ms sampling and a threshold of three, acceptance occurs roughly 10–15 ms after a change that then remains stable, depending on where the change falls between samples. This is an example, not a universal switch specification. Missed samples and scheduling jitter change the effective timing.

Sample once when due from a nonblocking loop, using the elapsed-time pattern. Do not run a rapid catch-up loop that reads the same level three times and pretends three time-spaced observations occurred. If elapsed stability is your requirement, timestamp the candidate's most recent observed change and accept only after the chosen interval.

Do not call a blocking delay inside the GPIO ISR. Instead, record that an edge occurred and defer filtering, or use a periodic sampler. An edge-only design must arrange a later observation even if no further edges arrive. Coordinate ISR/foreground state safely; volatile is not a queue or a lock.

Two different features: debouncing decides when a state is credible. Long-press, auto-repeat, and double-click recognition interpret already-debounced events with separate state machines.
SECTION 04

Test the uncomfortable inputs

  • A steady release and a steady press: no extra events while held.
  • A bouncing press and release: one press and one release after qualification.
  • A short pulse below the acceptance threshold: no accepted press.
  • Reset while held, missed sample deadlines, and a tick counter wrapping around.

Use synthetic sample traces to test the filter before touching hardware. Then measure the real switch if you need a justified threshold. An RC network and a suitable Schmitt-trigger input can help, but check the input's electrical requirements and timing; a capacitor alone is not a general safety or noise solution.

Hardware reference: use the NUCLEO-F401RE board documents to verify B1's connection and the device documentation for input specifications.
