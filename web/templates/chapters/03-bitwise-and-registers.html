SECTION 01

A register is just 32 switches

A hardware register is a row of bits living at a fixed memory address. Each bit is a tiny switch wired to something real — a pin, a mode, an enable. To control the chip, you flip the right switches without disturbing the others.

Three operations do almost everything. Each uses a mask — a number with 1s in the positions you care about — and a bitwise operator:

  • Set a bit: reg |= (1 << n) — OR forces the bit to 1.
  • Clear a bit: reg &= ~(1 << n) — AND with an inverted mask forces it to 0.
  • Toggle a bit: reg ^= (1 << n) — XOR flips it.
Why masks instead of just assigning reg = 0x20? Because a direct write clobbers all 32 bits. Read-modify-write with a mask touches only what you mean to.
